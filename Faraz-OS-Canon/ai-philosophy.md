## AI Philosophy

1. AI is the default execution layer of Faraz OS.
AI is not an optional assistant attached to the system; it is a core operational layer embedded into workflows.

2. AI is designed to absorb and execute a significant portion of human operational work.
In some bounded parts of the system, AI may replace a human role entirely or perform a major share of that role’s workload.

3. AI is responsible for operational acceleration and scalable execution, not unrestricted authority.
Its role is to perform repetitive, heavy, and context-dependent work at scale, while final judgment remains human-controlled in sensitive, high-impact, or ambiguous situations.

4. AI must act through structured context, not isolated prompts.
It must operate using memory, rules, workflows, domain boundaries, and system context rather than ad-hoc prompt-by-prompt improvisation.

5. AI autonomy must be bounded and policy-governed.
Direct external actions are only allowed through approved workflows, defined permissions, policies, and human checkpoints when required.

6. Faraz OS must be multi-model and provider-agnostic.
The system must support multiple model providers, model tiers, and routing strategies across paid, free, premium, and fallback options.

7. Model routing is a core architectural capability.
The system should select models dynamically based on task type, cost, quality, latency, availability, and risk.

8. Agent orchestration is part of the philosophy, not an implementation detail.
Faraz OS should support agents, subagents, and orchestrated execution patterns where specialized components collaborate inside governed workflows.

9. AI must improve through feedback loops.
Every correction, approval, rejection, and outcome should improve prompts, routing logic, workflows, evaluation, and memory over time.

10. AI exists to increase human leverage.
Its purpose is to compress operational workload, expand execution capacity, and elevate humans toward strategy, supervision, and quality control.
